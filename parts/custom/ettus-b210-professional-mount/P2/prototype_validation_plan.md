# Prototype validation plan — P2 preliminary B210 external cradle

**Status: proposed tests; none performed.** This plan applies to both the flat and upright variants of the machined, unwelded 6061 external cradle on the preliminary rigid 6 mm support plate. The shared cradle base is 160 × 180 mm. The upright version adds two machined 6061 L-supports with 6 mm ribbed web/foot thickness, 36 mm finished width and R6 internal roots, profiled from plate stock with machining allowance. Its nominal support footprint is 79 × 180 mm and overall height 176 mm. Use final drawings if those dimensions change. This plan does not authorize vehicle, airborne, outdoor, sustained vibration, overhead, or safety-critical use. Complete the installation requirements and repeat the affected checks if the mounting orientation, support, device configuration, hardware, loads, or cables change.

## Basis and acceptance record

The initial application is stationary indoor service with the supporting plate horizontal: the radio lies flat in one variant and stands upright on the two adapter feet in the other. Qualify each variant separately; a successful flat test does not qualify the upright bend or cantilever load path. Testing opposite load directions is a retention check, not a qualification for continuous inverted or overhead operation. Record CAD/source revision, drawing revision, material certification, part serials, device serial/configuration, as-built dimensions, hardware lots, locking method, mounting surface, orientation, fixture photographs, instrument IDs, readings, and pass/fail decisions.

The unconfirmed design inputs are a **1.00 kg maximum device mass allowance**, a conservative sum of **3g handling inertia plus 1g gravity**, and a **20 N cable force acting 50 mm from its reaction**, giving **1.0 N·m**. At g = 9.81 m/s², inertia plus gravity is 39.24 N; a co-directed 20 N cable force gives 59.24 N, rounded to a **60 N device handling force plus 1.0 N·m moment**. The upright complete-assembly allowance is **2.20 kg**, including device and mount. Calculated mount mass is approximately 0.900 kg (CAD estimate), making the expected 1 kg-radio package approximately 1.900 kg (estimate); actual weighing is required. The corresponding 4g plus 20 N is 106.33 N, rounded to **110 N horizontal and 110 N vertical concurrently, plus 1.0 N·m** for a conservative handling envelope. These independent component maxima exceed a single 3g vector; do not describe them as a standard acceleration test. The upright 110 N package case already includes mount mass and replaces, rather than adds to, the 60 N device case for adapter sizing.

Normal operation uses **1g gravity plus 20 N cable disturbance and 1.0 N·m** in the actual installation directions. The proposed static mount proof is **90 N/1.5 N·m for the cradle** and **150 N horizontal plus 150 N vertical plus 1.5 N·m for the upright adapter/package**. Cradle proof force is 1.5 times its device handling force; upright proof force is approximately 1.36 times the rounded 110 N component handling limit, and its cable couple is 1.5 times the handling couple. These are selected prototype screens, not a vibration standard or the analytical safety factor. The engineering note's final load positions and additional body-force equivalents govern fixture setup; separately supported mount mass is not to be silently omitted or counted twice.

Use a load cell/force gauge with approximately 1 N resolution, a torque arm of measured length, dial indicators with 0.01 mm resolution, feeler gauges, and a caliper or CMM suitable for the drawing tolerances. A 100 mm arm needs 10 N for 1.0 N·m or 15 N for 1.5 N·m. Account for fixture mass, gravity, and measured force direction so they are included once. Photograph the fixture and calculate the actual wrench at the contact region before testing.

## 1. Receiving inspection and real-device fit

1. Inspect material/finish certificates and all fabricated dimensions against the released prototype drawings. Check burrs, exposed sharp edges, corner radii, hole locations, seating flatness, hardware head/washer seating, full nut or female-thread engagement, screw tip position, and tool access. Resolve any discrepancy before using the radio as a fit gauge. Do not select assembly torque from an assumed generic diameter table; record the joint-specific setting or tightening procedure approved for the actual fastener/finish/locking combination.
2. Weigh the complete radio in its intended configuration, including installed options. Document the supported fraction of cable mass separately. Acceptance: radio mass does not exceed 1.00 kg and no added accessory changes the assumed center of gravity or lever arm. A heavier or appreciably offset assembly requires recalculation.
3. Measure the actual **lower pan contact width and length**, outer shell width, pan-contact-plane-to-top height, locations and protrusion of every foot, case seam/steps, end-panel lower rim, label boundaries, panel fasteners, connectors, and K-Slots. Use the final drawing datums and record measurement locations. A dimension over connector tips is not the same as a chassis contact dimension. Inspect multiple places along each pan edge; seams or coatings can control fit. Sample at least three radios if the mount is intended for a batch; otherwise label the fit as demonstrated for one serial number.
4. With the radio unpowered and all cables disconnected, lower it into the cradle. It must seat without force, scratching, feet removal, case disassembly, or device modification. Inspect the machined hard support lands (nominal CAD elevation z = 5.5 mm) and **installed anti-mar PET film thickness ≤0.20 mm** (catalog sheet nominal0.127 mm; adhesive inclusion unconfirmed), including adhesive if present. This thin film is a surface protector; it is not a compliant structural pad. Feet must remain clear of intended hard seating surfaces/pockets as designed; do not count adhesive feet as structural retention. Confirm that low end lips (nominally 2.1 mm above the pan, mount Z = 7.8 mm) contact only the intended metal pan region below labels. The retaining edge must preserve the specified ≤0.10 mm edge break. Check 2.3 mm lateral-stop engagement against the actual seam/step. Require broad contact with no loading of connector shells, PCB, lightpipes, labels, or unsupported panel features. Use removable witness film or thin paper strips for the contact map; remove all additional test film afterward.
5. Install both 5 mm upper retainers on their nominal 30 mm spacers against mechanical hard stops. Measure the installed top film, ≤0.20 mm total, including adhesive. Select spacers/shims until the **measured unloaded top clearance is 0.20–0.30 mm at every intended retention location**, cold and warm. Nominal CAD gap is 0.2508 mm and does not by itself pass this measured limit. Check both ends of each bar using feeler gauges without forcing the cover. Tightening retainer screws must not reduce the clearance by bending a bar into the radio. Record individual spacer/shim thicknesses and all gaps. This is a capture mount; intentional top-cover preload is not acceptable.
6. Verify side/end gaps against the drawing, checking both ends and both sides. Set the radio against each positive stop by hand and verify that top overlap and stop engagement persist at every extreme. Record maximum free translation in all three axes and rocking at the corners. No geometrically possible escape path, trapped connector, or tool obstruction is acceptable. If case variation prevents the specified gaps, revise parameters or approved shims; do not force the fit.

**Gate:** the lower end-stop/contact region remains provisional until this physical inspection demonstrates the intended pan contact. Nominal STEP contact or an attractive assembled view cannot establish enclosure strength or contact suitability.

For the upright adapters, inspect the actual machined 6 mm ribbed web/foot sections, R6 roots, perpendicularity, seating faces, and material certificate. Require **5.90 mm minimum finished web/foot thickness**; the nominal strength margin does not permit the general ±0.20 mm tolerance on these sections. These supports are machined, not formed: no bend coupon, K-factor, or developed flat pattern applies. Check cutter access, tool marks at the root, burrs and minimum finished section. Verify adapter hole positions, nominally at Z = 26/166 mm for cradle attachment and X = 20/55 mm in the feet, against the final drawing and machined datums; do not force misaligned brackets together. The cradle transformation for this variant is a 90° Y rotation followed by a 96 mm Z translation. Check this orientation using actual connector positions before assembly.

## 2. Connector, cable, installation, and service checks

Install the actual intended USB 3.0 Standard-B, SMA, power, timing/GPS and other used cable assemblies one at a time and together. Record manufacturer and part number for each plug/cable, plug dimensions, mating travel, required wrench/key envelope, minimum bend radius from that cable manufacturer's documentation, route, strain relief location, and swept space during connection/removal. If the vendor gives no minimum bend radius, obtain guidance or use a straight unrestricted route for the prototype; the CAD clearance envelope is not a cable specification.

Acceptance: every connector mates and unmates fully, latch/threads/tools remain accessible, no cable presses on a retainer or case edge, all bends meet the applicable cable specification, LEDs remain visible, and no cable carries device weight. Secure the cable bundle to the support independently so the radio is not the cable anchor. A 20 N design disturbance is not an allowable connector load or permission to test by pulling a cable.

Install and remove each variant from the support plate with the actual drivers, washers, and underside access. For the upright version **attach the adapter feet to the support before installing the cradle**, then verify access to all cradle-to-adapter screws. Check adapter seating without rocking, webs without twist, and the entire support-plate footprint under the feet. Demonstrate radio removal along the intended free direction after unplugging cables and removing the retainers; confirm headroom and a safe lifting grip. Secure the upright radio by hand or a temporary cradle support before removing its retainers. Demonstrate enclosure-panel servicing only with the radio removed and unplugged. No case screw needs to be loosened merely to remove the mount.

Perform **10 complete unplugged removal/reinstallation cycles**, inspecting at cycles 1, 5, and 10. Replace locking consumables according to their supplier's reuse instructions. At cycle 10, fit/clearance criteria must still pass, threads must engage freely, screws/retainers must remain fully seated, and there must be no burrs, stripped threads, loose inserts, damaged feet, case dents, label wear, or new cable interference.

## 3. Mount-only structural test using a surrogate

First use a rigid surrogate with the device's external contact envelope, equivalent center of gravity, and **1.00 kg surrogate mass**, in addition to actual mount mass. Confirm complete assembly mass is no more than 2.20 kg for the upright package and its CG is no higher than 120 mm or farther than 50 mm from the upright web datum. Its loading attachment must distribute forces through the cradle's intended contact surfaces. Keep it clear of foot pockets and do not create a stronger alternative mount load path. Bolt each variant to the actual 6 mm support-plate prototype with the specified hardware and conduct the full schedule separately. Place a catch/support beneath the rig, outside its normal load path. In the upright variant, instrument the top of the cradle, adapter roots, feet, and foot-to-plate joints to distinguish cantilever bending from bolt/joint movement.

Measure movement between the mount and support separately from movement of the captured surrogate. Apply loads smoothly; impact/drop loading is outside this test. Run at least the following:

| Test | Applied test load at the surrogate/contact reference | Hold/repetition |
|---|---|---|
| Normal operation, each variant | 1g actual gravity plus 20 N cable-equivalent force and 1.0 N·m in the proposed cable route's governing directions | 60 s; three ramps |
| Cradle handling translation | 60 N in each of ±X, ±Y, and ±Z, with fixture gravity included | 60 s each; three ramps to full load |
| Cradle handling rotation/combined | 1.0 N·m about each axis in both senses; then 60 N plus 1.0 N·m in the governing contact direction | 60 s each |
| Upright package handling | 110 N horizontal plus 110 N vertical plus 1.0 N·m concurrently at the engineering calculation's load positions | 60 s; repeat distinct governing senses |
| Cradle proof | 90 N plus 1.5 N·m in the governing combination; repeat distinct governing contacts/retainers | 60 s |
| Upright package proof | 150 N horizontal plus 150 N vertical plus 1.5 N·m at the same package reference; do not bypass adapter/foot load path | 60 s |

Compare readings with analytical predicted deflections. Provisional acceptance: no release, cracked part, torn ligament, stripped thread, fastener rotation, support separation, loss of overlap, or permanent distortion. Elastic mount-to-support movement after free clearance is taken up must be **≤0.75 mm at normal operation load**, **≤1.50 mm at the conservative handling envelope**, and **≤2.00 mm at the simultaneous 150 N package proof envelope**; residual mount deformation after unloading must be **≤0.10 mm**, including after proof. These are project serviceability targets, not manufacturer limits. The upright analytical proof estimate is 1.185 mm before bolt/support compliance; measure and resolve any excess rather than declaring it harmless because strength passes. Reserve at least 2 mm extra moving-assembly clearance within the installation, beyond actual plug/bend/tool clearances. Captured-body displacement includes designed free play: it must not exceed the measured pre-test free travel plus the applicable elastic limit or approach a retention-escape path. Relative lift within the cradle must preserve the separately calculated end-lip overlap; global upright cantilever motion is not extra allowed local lift. Recheck all fit and hardware criteria afterward. If the analytical design or available connector/cable space imposes a tighter limit, use that tighter value.

On the surrogate only, repeat the retention-escape check with the anti-mar films omitted to represent the drawing's bounded film-loss condition. Add the upper and lower film thickness losses to the measured lift clearance and confirm that low end lips and lateral stops retain positive overlap at the extreme lift/rocking position. Record the minimum remaining engagement; it must meet the final analytical retention requirement. Film loss must not cause release even though continued service without the film is not intended. Restore films and recheck gaps before placing the real radio.

The surrogate test checks the fabricated mount and its attachment. **It does not verify the B210 enclosure, feet, cover, or panel strength.**

## 4. Actual-device contact and retention test

After the surrogate test passes, repeat physical contact inspection using the unpowered, unplugged radio. Use a broad, padded, case-safe external loading fixture aligned with the intended pan/contact regions; it must not touch connectors, controls, labels, exposed components, or apply a point force to an unsupported cover span. Demonstrate the fixture on the surrogate first. The fixture itself must not bridge directly to the mount and bypass the radio-to-mount interface.

Inspect after successive **10, 20, 40, and 60 N** force levels and **0.25, 0.50, and 1.0 N·m** moment levels. Use each direction that loads a different stop, retainer, or contact region, in both variants. Hold the full proposed device handling level for 60 s after safe progression. Stop at the first visible dent, oil-canning, scrape, increasing residual movement, seam change, connector movement, or abnormal sound; document the limiting load and revise the contact design. The 110 N package and 150 N package-proof fixtures are surrogate-only tests; do not apply those package forces entirely through the actual radio. Do not apply the 90 N cradle proof load to the radio by default.

Acceptance at the full proposed device handling load: capture remains positive, movement meets the criteria above, unloaded top gap remains 0.20–0.30 mm, no visible or measurable residual case deformation above **0.10 mm** at monitored locations, seams remain unchanged, panels still service normally, and the radio passes its pre-test functional check afterward. Record the case measurement repeatability; a measurement method unable to resolve 0.10 mm cannot substantiate that criterion. If a safe case fixture/contact region cannot be established, or the full proposed load cannot be demonstrated without risking case damage, retain the **preliminary/unqualified** status and obtain enclosure load guidance or redesign the contact interface.

## 5. Thermal operation and warm fit

Use a representative application workload with simultaneous TX/RX, actual sample rates and duty cycle, intended power source, optional GPSDO if installed, cables, neighboring equipment, support plate, final orientation, and available airflow. RF operation must use appropriate test loads/connections and the application's normal limits. Test **both flat and upright orientations** separately. Establish a baseline with the same radio unmounted in the same orientation and environment, safely supported without adding a cooling path, then repeat mounted. Log ambient air near the radio, case temperatures at multiple repeatable points, stream errors, dropouts, resets, and relevant application metrics. Do not presume an internal temperature sensor exists or prescribe an unsupported maximum case temperature.

Test at normal indoor ambient and near the upper intended ambient while staying within the manufacturer's **0–40 °C** range and **10–90% non-condensing humidity**. A chamber target of 38 °C with bounded overshoot below 40 °C is preferable to unintentionally exceeding the limit. Run at least two hours and until all monitored case temperatures vary by less than 1 °C over 30 minutes; extend if equilibrium is not reached. Repeat the same baseline/mounted protocol at the warm condition.

Acceptance: no new application errors/resets or functional degradation attributable to the mount; no clearance loss, forced cover contact, film migration, loosened joint, or hot cable interference. A **mounted-versus-baseline case-temperature rise ≤5 °C** is a provisional comparative design target, not an Ettus temperature allowance or proof of component temperature margin. A larger increase requires thermal review and design/workload/airflow revision. Record all readings; merely remaining below a comfortable touch temperature is not a valid criterion.

With the radio safely unplugged after thermal exposure, check accessible fit promptly without disturbing the established seating. Demonstrate 0.20–0.30 mm top gaps at the warm operating condition, and after cooling to the minimum intended indoor condition (never outside the device rating or into condensation). Inspect coating/contact wear, film adhesion/creep, and spacer/shim seating. If the intended installation spans a wider temperature range than this prototype test, repeat the fit and operating checks at its actual extremes.

## Release decision

Archive the completed measurement sheets, load/displacement curves, contact photographs, thermal traces, functional logs, and post-test inspection. Close every failure with a revised drawing/source revision and repeat affected tests. Prototype acceptance is limited to the measured device/configuration, support, orientation, cables, load envelope, and temperature range. It is not a fatigue, vibration, drop, environmental, EMI/grounding, or regulatory certification. Establish those requirements and a suitable test program separately before claiming a broader application.
